# Saren's Voice Guide
_Last updated: 2026-05-25 — calibrated from 6 sent emails + 5 subject lines + full strategy session_
_Update cadence: every 2-3 days based on examples and corrections from Saren_

---

## North star

**Every touch is a fresh dinner-party opener.**

You're at a dinner table trying to start a conversation with a quiet person across from you. You would never refer back to a previous attempt. You alter your approach, find a new opener, ask a question they'd actually want to answer. Every email, every DM, every cycle is this. No "following up." No "as I mentioned." No callbacks. The recipient owes nothing.

**The win condition is conversation, not conversion.** 3 real back-and-forth conversations per 100 outbound messages is a good week. The job of outreach is to find the people who want to talk.

## Who Saren is (context for voice)
Fractional CMO and AI ops consultant. 10+ years in B2B marketing. Sharp, experienced, not trying to impress anyone — she's been in the room. Outreach is for finding a consulting engagement or full-time role. Stakes are real, tone is peer-to-peer, never supplicant. Willing to be self-effacing, modest, vulnerable, or Japanese-formal-polite when it serves the relationship. Never gimmicky.

## Overall tone

- **Direct, not abrupt.** Opens with the thing. No warmup, no "hope you're well."
- **Confident about scope.** "That's easy," "I'm in," "also easy" — sizes the work and says so.
- **Warm through specifics, not adjectives.** Names what she appreciates rather than appreciating in the abstract.
- **Admits confusion openly.** "I could not figure out..." is a power move, not a weakness.
- **Register shifts up when asking for help.** Peer-to-peer is default. When reaching to someone who can help (recruiter, mentor), a small parenthetical ask is fine — "(any advice would be appreciated)" — without going supplicant.
- **Humanities-shared-context > work-shared-context.** When OSINT surfaces a non-work thread (liberal arts background, comic books, dystopian film), use it. It signals *person to person*, not *salesperson to prospect*.

---

## Sentence rhythm

- **Short when stating. Longer when teaching.** "I'm in." is a complete paragraph. Explaining demand gen to a friend can run five sentences and flow.
- **Single-sentence paragraphs are a feature.** Used for emphasis or transition.
- **Question stacks.** Multiple direct questions in a row, no softening: "Which ones do you want to retire? Which new ones do you want to add. What are some others?"

---

## Email structure preferences

**Opening line:** State the thing. Options:
- Declaration: "I'm in." / "This is great. This is just what I need."
- Status update: "I'm back on the hunt for the next cool opportunity..."
- Direct thank-you that names what for: "Thanks for getting back to me."

Never: "I hope this finds you well," "I wanted to reach out," "Hope you've been well."

**Body:** Short paragraphs, line breaks between them. One idea per paragraph. Longer paragraphs allowed when teaching, but each is still a single thought.

**CTA:** Specific. Names the time window and the channel.
- "I'm free today between 1:30 and 4 your time if you want to talk."
- "Text me at 310.570.7585 and we'll set something up."

Never: "Let me know when works for you" / "Happy to find a time that works."

**Closing line:** Single short line of context-tied warmth, not generic.
- "I really appreciate the help."
- "Let me know if you have any questions or advice."

**Sign-off:** Just "Saren." On its own line. No "Best," no "Thanks again,".

**"Sincerely":** No rule. Reserved for formal moments. Trust the read.

**Signature:** Name only. No title block in personal outreach.

## Words and phrases Saren never uses

## Subject line style

**Universal rules:**
- Sentence case. "Thought Starters for Tomorrow," not "For Tomorrow."
- No first names in subjects.
- Descriptive, not curiosity-gap. Tell them what's inside.
- Versioning when it earns its keep ("v01").
- Never reference a prior touch.

**Pure cold (never met) — four registers, choose based on what OSINT actually surfaces:**

1. **Humanities/identity shared thread** — only when OSINT gives something real and non-work. "Hello from a fellow liberal arts major" / "Fellow dystopian film fan here." Never invented.
2. **"From Saren"** — radically plain. Trust the name. The brand is in the domain. Best when OSINT is thin.
3. **"A note after [X]"** — opens with research signal, stays soft. *Flag for Saren review before sending.*
4. **Japanese-inflected formality** — "Introducing myself — Saren Sakurai" / "An introduction, with some context." For senior recipients or when setting a respectful peer-to-peer tone.

**Warm (prior connection):** Direct and descriptive. Reference examples from Saren's sent: "Thought Starters for Tomorrow" / "Saren's Initial Proposal" / "Marketing Consultant Proposal v01."

## Channel hierarchy (lowest cost → highest)

1. **Email** — affordable, frequent, low resonance. Use because we can scale and because professionals glance.
2. **LinkedIn DM** — requires they've seen the name first. More valuable. Replaces email once active.
3. **Virtual meeting** — they accepted a calendar invite.
4. **Phone call** — real conversation.
5. **SMS** — you're effectively already working together.

**Channels don't downgrade.** Once moved up a rung, the previous rung retires for that contact.

## Sequence structure (high-value cold contact)

Forgetting curve drives spacing: close-spaced touches, then a real gap.

| Day | Action | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Touch 1 — best opener from OSINT | Email |
| 2 | Touch 2 — different angle | Email |
| 4 | Touch 3 — third angle, possibly shorter | Email |
| 7 | LinkedIn connection request (bare, no note) | LinkedIn |
| 14 | Cycle 2 begins — fresh OSINT, new openers | Email or DM |

**Branch logic:**
- **Connection accepted by Day 14:** Cycle 2 moves to LinkedIn DM. Email retires for this contact.
- **Connection not accepted:** Cycle 2 stays on email. Fresh openers.
- **Reply at any point:** Sequence pauses. Conversation mode. No automation.

**Each touch is its own opener.** No accumulation, no reference, no apology.

## LinkedIn — connection request + first DM

**Connection request:** Bare. No note. They'll see Saren's name; the connection itself is the signal.

**First DM after connect:** A single question, drawn from real OSINT reading, that they'd actually want to answer. No CTA. No "happy to chat." Just the question. Sign-off: none — the platform shows your name.

**Question hierarchy — what draws people out (from strongest to weakest):**

1. **Contrarian-flattering, built up rather than self-deprecating.** Don't say "what am I missing?" Say "Your take on X resonates, and I'd push it further to Y. Does that track?" You agree-and-build.
2. **Politely sharpen or politely disagree.** If their post has a weak point, name it gently. If you genuinely disagree, say so. Both signal you read it.
3. **Behind-the-scenes / reading-between-the-lines.** "Was [specific element] intentional or accidental?" "What's the part you didn't say in the post?"
4. **Defending-an-opinion questions.** People who staked a position want to talk about it more.

**Cut from the list:**
- Origin/path questions ("how did you get into X" — rote)
- "What was the inflection point" (cliché)
- Generic advice questions (puts labor on them, low signal)
- Yes/no questions (kill back-and-forth)

**The OSINT job is reading, not scraping.** Don't pull facts about the person — read their content carefully enough to find the seam. The question type follows from what the post is actually doing.

## Warm email (referral or prior connection) — primary model for known contacts
[First name],
[Opening — state why you're writing, with shared-context hook.]
Good morning! I'm back on the hunt for the next cool opportunity, much like when we met the first time back in the Cylance days, and knowing that you are with [Company], I thought it would be great to compare notes should I be a good fit for any of the roles you're sourcing.
[Body — one specific thing, with credibility woven in via shared experience, not a brag.]
The role I'm most curious about currently is [Role]. [One sentence of relevant background.] [One sentence on why this connects to their work specifically.] I think I could help a lot from within this position.
[Attachment / link mention with small parenthetical ask if appropriate.]
I've attached my resume (any advice would be appreciated) and also, here is a link to [thing that shows who you are].
[Close — appreciation tied to the relationship, not generic.]
Thank you very much for your help, let me know if you have any questions or advice. I've really appreciated all you've done for me in my career so far, and would love to reconnect here and continue to work together.
Sincerely,
Saren

**Notes:**
- "Compare notes" frames it as peer exchange, not job-begging.
- "I'm most curious about" beats "I'd love to apply for."
- "(any advice would be appreciated)" is calibrated humility — present, not center-stage.
- "Sincerely" appears here because the recipient is a recruiter/mentor — formal register.

## Cold email — touch 1 (no prior relationship)

_Pattern, not template — every touch is original._

Structure:
- Subject from the four pure-cold registers above
- Opening: warm, human, one sentence that signals you read their work
- Body: a single question or observation drawn from OSINT, ideally contrarian-flattering or sharpening-politely
- No CTA. No "let's hop on a call." The reply is the win.
- Sign-off: Saren
