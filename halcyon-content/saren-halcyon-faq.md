## Q10: "What would you ask him if you were on the hiring committee?"

These are the questions that surface his actual thinking — not his prepared answers:

1. **"Walk me through the last time your pipeline number was wrong. What happened and what did you change?"** — This surfaces whether he owns outcomes or explains them away. The best demand gen leaders have a specific, unflattering story here.

2. **"What's your read on Halcyon's current positioning in the market? Where do you think the message is landing and where is it falling short?"** — This tests his pre-work and his willingness to be direct with a prospective employer. Candidates who haven't done the research can't answer this. Candidates who have but are afraid to challenge will hedge.

3. **"How would you structure a demand gen team of five people for Halcyon 18 months from now? What roles, in what order, and why?"** — This is an operational intelligence test. The answer reveals whether he thinks in systems or in individual hires.

4. **"Describe the worst-performing campaign you've ever run. What was the diagnosis?"** — Same logic as question 1. Accountability and analytical honesty are the traits you're evaluating, not just wins.

5. **"6sense is already in your stack. How would you use it differently than it's probably being used today?"** — A tool-specific question that tests whether his ABM knowledge is real or performative. He should have a specific, technical answer about intent signal prioritization, account tiering, and campaign trigger configuration.

6. **"What does Halcyon's demand gen need to look like in 36 months for a board to say the marketing function delivered?"** — This tests his ability to think in outcomes, not activities. The answer should include pipeline metrics, CAC trajectory, market share indicators, and category positioning — not campaign counts.
