# GPT-5 Prompting Cheat Sheet


Optimised for reasoning, writing, coding, and creative tasks

⸻

1. Deep Reasoning & Problem-Solving

PROMPT: You are a [role]. Break this problem into clear, logical steps before producing the answer. Think hard about the solution.

EXAMPLE: You are a business strategist. Break down the steps to launch an AI-powered compliance tool in the UK market before providing the final go-to-market strategy. Think hard about the solution.

★ WHY IT WORKS: Forces GPT-5 to plan, not just answer, reducing reasoning errors.

⸻

2. “Critique Before Final” Mode

PROMPT: First, write your answer. Then, review it critically and note any weaknesses. Finally, rewrite it to address those weaknesses.

EXAMPLE: First, write a 400-word press release announcing a new sustainability initiative. Then, critique it for clarity and persuasion, and rewrite it accordingly.

★ WHY IT WORKS: Leverages GPT-5’s improved self-reflection abilities.

⸻

3. Role + Objective + Constraints

PROMPT: You are [expert role]. Your goal is to [goal]. Follow these constraints: [rules].

EXAMPLE: You are a senior policy advisor. Your goal is to summarise a 20-page climate report for a parliamentary committee. Constraints: max 500 words, neutral tone, bullet-point format.

★ WHY IT WORKS: GPT-5 sticks to constraints better than earlier models.

⸻

4. Progressive Deepening

PROMPT: First, give a high-level overview of [topic]. Then, expand on the most important point in depth, with evidence and examples.

EXAMPLE: First, summarise the current state of AI regulation in the EU. Then, expand on the AI Act’s enforcement challenges.

★ WHY IT WORKS: Builds depth without overwhelming the first output.

⸻

5. Multi-Format Integration

PROMPT: Here is a mix of inputs: [paste text, attach image, or reference file]. Analyse them together and produce [output type].

EXAMPLE: Here’s a market report PDF and a product photo. Analyse both and create a 2-slide pitch for investors.

★ WHY IT WORKS: GPT-5 is better at merging multiple modalities into one coherent output.

⸻

6. Exploratory “Idea Map”

PROMPT: Explore all possible ways for [broad goal]. Organise them into categories, and for each category, list at least 3 concrete examples or approaches.

EXAMPLE: Explore all possible ways to monetise a newsletter about AI tools. Organise into sponsorships, products, community, and partnerships.

★ WHY IT WORKS: Uses GPT-5’s breadth while keeping it structured.

⸻

7. Expert Emulation

PROMPT: Answer as if you were [famous expert/professional role], applying their style, decision-making process, and priorities.

EXAMPLE: Answer as if you were Elon Musk deciding how to launch a new AI chip startup.

★ WHY IT WORKS: GPT-5’s style simulation is much sharper than earlier models.

⸻

8. Constraint Anchoring for Creativity

PROMPT: Create [output] with these constraints: [list constraints].

EXAMPLE: Write a 100-word LinkedIn post explaining generative AI to a 10-year-old using only one metaphor throughout and ending with a question.

★ WHY IT WORKS: Constraints focus GPT-5’s creativity and avoid drift.

⸻

9. Long-Context Knowledge Recall

PROMPT: Using everything we’ve discussed so far in this chat, produce [output]. If needed, refer back to specific details mentioned earlier.

EXAMPLE: Using all the context from our earlier conversation about AI regulation, write a 2,000-word policy paper.

★ WHY IT WORKS: GPT-5 maintains detail over longer conversations.

⸻

10. Broad Goal to Precise Execution (2-Step)

PROMPT:
	1.	Here’s my broad goal: [goal]. Help me define the exact deliverable we need.
	2.	Now produce that deliverable according to the agreed definition.

EXAMPLE:
	1.	Broad goal: Improve my LinkedIn engagement on AI posts.
	2.	Deliverable: A 4-week content calendar with post ideas, hooks, and CTAs.

★ WHY IT WORKS: Broad exploration first, precision second.

⸻

PRO TIP for GPT-5: If you’re unsure how to phrase a request, you can literally say:

“I have a fuzzy goal. Help me shape it into a precise prompt before answering.”

GPT-5 will guide you to clarity before attempting execution.
