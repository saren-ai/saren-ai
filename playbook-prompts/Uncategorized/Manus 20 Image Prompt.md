# Manus 20 Image Prompt


Generate 20 unique old-school travel poster designs for a fictional destination called Japanifornia — a dreamlike fusion of California and Japan.

Word restriction:
	•	The only text on each poster should be the single word: Japanifornia.
	•	No dates, no taglines, no event language.

Theme:
Each poster should feel like it’s inviting someone to visit Japanifornia — whatever that means visually: Pacific surf meets Shinto shrines, neon Tokyo over Malibu sunsets, sakura drifting through Venice Beach skateparks, sushi bars glowing beside Joshua Tree boulders.

Style direction:
	•	Blend recognizably Californian aesthetics (mid-century modern travel posters, ’70s surf and skate art, Palm Springs pastel modernism, Hollywood Art Deco, psychedelic concert posters) with iconic Japanese styles (Edo-period ukiyo-e woodblock, Showa-era matchbox art, anime/manga film posters, Muji-style minimalism, contemporary streetwear graphics).
	•	Incorporate retro tourism poster energy but in a cross-cultural, imaginative way — hybrid landscapes, architecture, and iconography from both regions.
	•	Include a mix of vibrant, high-energy designs and serene, minimalist compositions.

Variation requirements:
Explore within these style categories:
* Mid-century modern travel posters
* Lowrider/Chicano muralism — bold outlines, vivid colors, cultural iconography
* Tiki culture graphics — bamboo typography, tropical exotica, mid-century escapism
* Case Study House modernism — clean lines, floor-to-ceiling glass, desert integration
* Hot rod pinstriping — flowing metallic lines, flame patterns, custom lettering
* California impressionism — plein air lighting, eucalyptus groves, coastal atmosphere
* Kawaii culture graphics — rounded forms, pastel palettes, adorable character design
* Sumi-e ink painting — expressive brushstrokes, negative space, monochromatic washes
* Traditional textile patterns — geometric sashiko stitching, asanoha hemp leaves, seigaiha waves
* Pachinko parlor neon — electric brightness, chrome details, sensory overload
* Meiji-era commercial lithography — Western-influenced Japanese advertising, ornate borders
* Bento box composition — organized compartments, balanced negative space, functional beauty
* Harajuku street fashion graphics — clashing patterns, pop culture mashups, rebellious color
* Japanese woodblock & Showa-era commercial art
* Surf/skate/punk street art
* Art Deco Hollywood
Minimalist, wabi-sabi inspired layouts
Vary layout, typography, illustration vs. photographic textures, and color palettes (e.g., sun-faded retro tones, neon brights, indigo + gold, soft pastel gradients).
Use retro printing effects, halftones, and vintage paper grain for authenticity.

Artistic references for style diversity:
* Jamie Hewlett – Gorillaz-style illustrated worlds with bold lines and urban edge
* David Nakayama – dynamic, pop-culture-infused comic book illustration with high color saturation
* Takashi Murakami – Superflat aesthetic with bright, flat planes of color and playful surrealism
* Tadanori Yokoo – Japanese psychedelic pop-art posters from the 1960s–70s
* David Hockney – bright, geometric California landscapes and pools
* E. McKnight Kauffer – classic Art Deco travel posters
* Hokusai – Edo-period woodblock print detailing and composition
* John Van Hamersveld – iconic 1960s California surf art and psychedelic poster design
* Rick Griffin — psychedelic surf poster master, flowing organic lettering
* Ed "Big Daddy" Roth — hot rod monster art, irreverent underground comics style
* Wayne Thiebaud — California pop art, thick paint application, everyday subject elevation
* Richard Diebenkorn — Bay Area Figurative movement, geometric color fields
* Robert Williams — lowbrow art pioneer, hyperdetailed surreal narratives
* Yusaku Kamekura — Olympic poster design, bold geometric modernism
* Shigeo Fukuda — optical illusion master, minimalist visual puzzles
* Kitagawa Utamaro — ukiyo-e portraiture, delicate line work, intimate domestic scenes
* Yoshitaka Amano — Final Fantasy concept art, ethereal watercolor fantasy aesthetics
* Kenya Hara — Muji design philosophy, radical simplicity, material honesty

Output:
Provide all 20 posters as a single ZIP file.
