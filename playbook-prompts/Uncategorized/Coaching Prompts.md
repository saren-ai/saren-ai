# Coaching Prompts


1

I want you to be my Socratic mentor for [specific goal]. Use the Socratic method to help me identify cognitive biases and blind spots in my current approach. Start by asking me the 3 most penetrating questions about my situation, and then guide our conversation using systematic doubt and metacognition frameworks. After each of my responses, highlight one assumption I'm making and help me pressure-test it.

2

Let's create a decision matrix for my 2025 goals. First, help me define 5 key metrics for success. Then, for each potential path forward, evaluate it through three lenses: a) antifragility (how it benefits from disorder),
b) regret minimization (Bezos framework), and c) ecological rationality (how well it fits my specific environment and constraint). Also identify which of these paths have positive spillover effects on other goals.

3

Act as my accountability partner using implementation intention protocols. For my goal of [specific goal], help me create 'if-then' scenarios following the format: 'If situation X arises, then I will do Y! However, I want you to specifically focus on second-order and third-order consequences, identifying cascade triggers that could either derail or accelerate my progress.
Then, help me design pre-commitments to handle these scenarios.

4

Let's use inverse planning for my 2025 goals. Start from December 2025 and work backwards, but with a twist - I want you to apply the 'Theory of Constraints' methodology. At each milestone working backwards, identify: 1) The main constraint holding progress back, 2) Two ways to exploit that constraint, 3) One way to subordinate other activities to that constraint, and 4) A way to elevate beyond that constraint. Create this timeline with specific quarterly markers.

5

Create a personal 'Antilibrary' for my growth in 2025. First, outline what I already know about [topic/goall. Then, using the Dunning-Kruger effect as a framework, map out my known unknowns and potential unknown unknowns. For each area of desired growth, provide me with: a) A question I should be asking but probably aren't, b) A contrarian viewpoint that challenges my current approach, and c)
Three adjacent disciplines that could provide unexpected insights.
