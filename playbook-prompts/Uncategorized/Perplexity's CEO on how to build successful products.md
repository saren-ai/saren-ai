# Perplexity's CEO on how to build successful products


(Source: YCombinator)
Aravind Srinivas started Perplexity with one goal: to rethink how we search, browse, and Interact with information orline. In this corversation, he shares the journey from hacking together a natural-language-to-SQl, search fool to bulding a product used by millions around the work.

He recently spoke to Y Combinator and shared his views on how to bulld successful products:

1. Speed and Innovation Are Critical

• The onty sustainabie advantage is speed - move faster than competitors, especially in fast-evolving fields like AJ. Product teams should prioritize rapid iteration and shipping new features quickly to stay ahead

2. User Experience is a Differentiator

• Obsess over user experience. Even when competing with giants, a product that is faster, more accurato, and easier to use can win usors. Strinivas credits a focus on user experience, inspired by Larry Page, as a key differentiator for Perplexity.

3. Accuracy and Trust Matter

• Bulking trust through accurate answers and minimizing hallucinations is essential, especially for Al products. Perplexty invests in internal benchmarks and better search indexes to reduce errors.

4. Brand and Narrative Are Strategic Assets

• Brand value and a clear narrative help products stand out, even when features are casy to replicate. Teams should communicate wiry their product exists and wha makes it unique, focusing on strengths like speed and accuracy.

5. Adaptability and Focus

• Be wiling to adapt your product vision as technology and user needs evolve, but avoid changing direction too frequently. Start with a strong idea, iterate quickly, and commit once you see real user traction.

6. Embrace Competition and Learn from It

• Expect that successful ideas will be copied by larger piayers. Use this as motivation to move faster and build a unique identity around your product.

7. Leverage Al Tools, but Maintain Core Skills

• Al coding tools can dramatically speed up development, but core engineering skills remain vital, especially for critical infrastructure. Product teams should balance adoption of new tools with maintaining technical expertise.

8. Partnerships and Integrations 
Create Stickiness

• Integrations with other platforms and services can create network effects and make your product more indispensable to users.
• Leverage network effects and strategic integrations to make your product stickier and more valuable.

9. Resilience and Motivation

• Building products is hard, and setbacks are inevitable. Staying motivated and persistent - "never give up"-is crucial for long-term success.
