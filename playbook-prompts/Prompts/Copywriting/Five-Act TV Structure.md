# Five-Act TV Structure


This isn't about commercial breaks anymore. It's about narrative pressure. Each act raises the stakes, twists the knife, and drives the emotional arc forward. Think of it like five turns of like tightening the screws until the final break.

ACT ONE: The Hook & Disruption

Purpose: Grab the audience by the throat and unseat the status quo.
Pages: ~1 - 12 (up to first act break)
• Open in motion. No warm-up lap.
• Show your protagonist in their element. Flawed, sharp, reactive.
• Drop the inciting incident: an event, shift, or reveal that sets today's problem in motion
• End with a turning point: things just got personal, complicated, or impossible to ignore
This act asks: "What the hell just happened?"

ACT TWO: The Complication
Purpose: Escalate the central conflict, both external and emotional
Pages: ~13 - 25
• Your character starts chasing a fix, a win, or control, but they're off-balance
• Introduce subplots and relationship pressure
• World starts closing in: rules change, alliances crack, time runs out
• Often ends with a complication or obstacle that deepens the mess
This act asks: "What am I really up against?"

ACT THREE: The Crisis
Purpose: Force the protagonist to confront what they're afraid of or refusing to see
Pages: ~26 - 37
• Everything intensifies. The emotional and external stakes start to bleed together.
Character's flaw, wound, or blind spot becomes a problem they can't dodge anymore
Major reversal, betrayal, or discovery
Usually ends with a personal blow not just tactical
This act asks: "What if I'm not strong enough to fix this?"

ACT FOUR: The Fallout
Purpose: The unraveling. The consequences hit. The reckoning begins.
Pages: ~38 - 47
• The character scrambles. Desperate move.
Retaliation. Spiral.
They burn a bridge. Say the wrong thing. Lose someone.
Lowest emotional point often lands here, but not always in Act 5.
• Ends with a sharp decision or shift in strategy.
Ready or not, it's go time.
This act asks: "What do I have left to lose?"

ACT Five: The Payoff
Purpose: Land the climax and show what it cost.
Pages: -48 - 60
• Final confrontation, plan, or escalation. The hammer drops.
• If it's a serialized arc: this sets up fallout for the next episode
• If it's self-contained: this resolves today's story and reveals growth, damage, or both
• End with a lingering emotion or question, not always a neat bow
This act asks: "What does this change and what's next?"

WHY USE FIVE ACTS?
• Perfect for TV - builds tension in modular chunks
• Gives space for character development and subplots
• Each act is its own arc - setup, escalation, decision
• Forces you to build momentum, not just meander
