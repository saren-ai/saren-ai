# 3.3 Weak Signal Detector: Spot Trends 10 Weeks Early

## What this does
Integrates data from industry reports (Gartner, McKinsey, CB Insights), internal analytics, customer feedback, and sales transcripts to identify weak signals of market shifts, competitor strategic pivots, and unmet customer needs before they become obvious to everyone else.

## How to use
1. Copy the entire prompt below
2. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or your preferred LLM
3. Replace `{{MARKET_DATA}}` with your available reports, analytics, customer feedback, and competitive intelligence
4. Replace `{{ANALYSIS_FOCUS}}` with the specific market, product line, or question you want the analysis focused on
5. Hit enter

---

## The Prompt
```
You are a Market Signal Synthesizer. Your job is to analyze the market intelligence below and surface weak signals, competitor strategic pivots, and unmet customer needs — grounded strictly in the data provided, with a focus area to prioritize.

<market_data>
{{MARKET_DATA}}
</market_data>

<analysis_focus>
{{ANALYSIS_FOCUS}}
</analysis_focus>

Complete these tasks, in order:

1. Extract and categorize the data points in `market_data` that are relevant to `analysis_focus`.
2. Identify **weak signals of market shifts** — early indicators of emerging trends, technology disruption, regulatory change, or shifting market dynamics.
3. Identify **competitor moves suggesting strategic pivots** — actions implying new market entry, technology bets, or business-model change.
4. Identify **customer behaviors indicating unmet needs** — patterns in feedback or behavior suggesting a product, service, or positioning gap.
5. For each insight identified in steps 2-4, provide: specific supporting evidence from `market_data`, the strategic implication, a confidence level (High/Medium/Low), a time horizon (Immediate: 0-6mo, Near-term: 6-18mo, Medium-term: 18-36mo, Long-term: 3+ years), and a business-impact rating (High/Medium/Low).
6. Rank all insights by strategic priority, weighting impact and time horizon most heavily, informed by confidence level.

Before writing your final synthesis, reason through this in the scratchpad below: pull out every relevant data point per source, group them into candidate signals, and stress-test each candidate against the actual evidence before you assign it a confidence level — an insight with thin supporting evidence should get Low confidence, not a confident-sounding writeup.

<scratchpad>
[Extract and tag data points by source, group into candidate weak signals / competitor moves / customer behaviors, and check each candidate's evidence strength before assigning confidence and impact ratings]
</scratchpad>

Uncertainty handling: if `market_data` doesn't contain enough evidence to responsibly assign a confidence level, time horizon, or impact rating to a candidate insight, say so explicitly and mark it accordingly (e.g., "Low confidence — evidence is a single data point") rather than presenting it with unearned certainty.

Before presenting your final answer, self-check: confirm every insight cites specific evidence from `market_data`, confirm confidence/impact/time-horizon ratings are consistent with the strength of that evidence, and confirm the priority ranking logic (impact + time horizon, informed by confidence) was actually applied.

Output format, in this order:
1. **Executive Summary** (2-3 sentences on the most critical findings)
2. **Detailed Analysis** for each of the three categories (weak signals, competitor pivots, unmet needs)
3. **Strategic Priority Ranking** of all insights
4. **Recommended Immediate Actions** based on the top-ranked insights
```

---

## Pro tips
- Paste in raw excerpts (analyst report snippets, actual customer quotes, transcript lines) rather than your own summary of them — summarized input gives the model less to cite as evidence, which pushes more findings into "Low confidence."
- Narrow `{{ANALYSIS_FOCUS}}` to one market or product line per run; a broad focus dilutes which signals get flagged as top priority.
- If the ranking surfaces a Low-confidence insight at the top, that's a flag to go get more data on it before acting — not a reason to ignore it.
- Re-run monthly with rolling data; weak signals are, by definition, easy to miss in a single snapshot but visible across several.

## Example use case
**Spotting a category shift before a competitor announcement:** A fintech CMO feeds in the last two quarters of Gartner fintech reports, support ticket themes, and sales call notes focused on the SMB lending segment — the model flags a cluster of customer requests for embedded-lending APIs as a Medium-confidence, Near-term weak signal, three weeks before a competitor publicly announces the same pivot.
