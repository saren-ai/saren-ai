# 1.3 Political Chess Master: Map Hidden Agendas

## What this does
Analyzes meeting notes and email threads to map hidden tensions, competing agendas, and unspoken concerns around a specific initiative. Identifies which stakeholders need pre-alignment and provides bridging language for each conversation.

## How to use
1. Copy the entire prompt below
2. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or your preferred LLM
3. Replace `{{INITIATIVE}}` with your specific project or decision
4. Replace `{{MEETING_NOTES_AND_EMAILS}}` with meeting notes, emails, or Slack threads related to this initiative
5. Hit enter

---

## The Prompt
```
You will be analyzing meeting notes and email threads to identify hidden tensions and competing agendas around a specific initiative. Your goal is to map the political landscape and provide strategic recommendations for stakeholder alignment.

Here are the meeting notes and email threads to analyze:

<meeting_notes_and_emails>
{{MEETING_NOTES_AND_EMAILS}}
</meeting_notes_and_emails>

The specific initiative you should focus on is:

<initiative>
{{INITIATIVE}}
</initiative>

Complete the following tasks in order:
1. Read all communications with the initiative above as the lens — every observation should tie back to it
2. Identify hidden tensions that are implied but not explicitly stated
3. Map competing agendas and priorities across stakeholders
4. Identify which stakeholders need a separate 1:1 alignment conversation before any group meeting, and why
5. Draft bridging language for each conversation that needs one

Before writing your final answer, work through your thinking in a scratchpad block. In it, reason through:
1. What are the different perspectives and positions expressed regarding the initiative?
2. What underlying concerns, motivations, or interests might be driving each stakeholder's position?
3. Where do you see direct conflicts or competing priorities, and where are tensions only implied?
4. Which stakeholders seem to have the most significant disagreements, and would a group conversation make that worse?
5. What common ground or shared interests can you identify to build bridging language from?

<scratchpad>
[Your analysis here]
</scratchpad>

If the communications don't give you enough signal to characterize a stakeholder's position or motivation with confidence, say so explicitly and state that you're inferring rather than quoting — do not invent a stakeholder's opinion or a conversation that isn't in the source material.

Before presenting your final answer, verify every tension, agenda, and alignment recommendation you name traces back to something in the communications above or a clearly labeled inference, and that your response uses all four output tags below, in order.

Provide your final analysis using exactly this structure:

<hidden_tensions>
List and describe the hidden tensions you've identified around the initiative. Focus on underlying conflicts that may not be explicitly stated but can be inferred from the communications.
</hidden_tensions>

<competing_agendas>
Map out the different agendas and priorities of various stakeholders. Explain how these agendas conflict with or compete against each other regarding the initiative.
</competing_agendas>

<stakeholder_alignment_needs>
Identify which specific stakeholders need separate alignment conversations before any group meeting. Explain why these particular stakeholders need individual attention and what issues need to be addressed with each.
</stakeholder_alignment_needs>

<bridging_language>
Suggest specific language, phrases, or framing approaches that could help bridge the gaps between conflicting positions. Provide language that acknowledges different concerns while moving toward common ground.
</bridging_language>
```

---

## Pro tips
- Include the messiest, most unfiltered threads you have — polished summary notes strip out the tension this prompt is built to find
- Name the initiative narrowly in `{{INITIATIVE}}` ("Q3 pricing restructure," not "pricing") so the model doesn't spread its analysis across unrelated topics
- Treat the `bridging_language` section as a first draft — read it aloud before you use it in an actual 1:1
- If the model repeatedly says it's "inferring" on a given stakeholder, that's a signal you need more of their communications, not a model failure

## Example use case
**Before a contentious re-org announcement:** Paste in three months of email threads and meeting notes about the initiative → get a map of which two VPs need separate pre-briefs before the all-hands, and language to use with each.
