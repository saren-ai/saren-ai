# 3.1 Scenario Engine: Model Five Futures Simultaneously

## What this does
Builds five probability-weighted scenarios for a strategic decision — each modeling market volatility, competitor responses, regulatory shifts, and black-swan events together — then attaches early-warning signals and a trigger-based read to each one.

## How to use
1. Copy the entire prompt below
2. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or your preferred LLM
3. Replace `{{DECISION}}` with your specific strategic decision
4. Replace `{{CONTEXT}}` with relevant market data, risk models, competitive intel, and current situation
5. Hit enter

---

## The Prompt
```
You are a scenario-planning analyst. Your job is to build five distinct, probability-weighted scenarios for the strategic decision below, using only the decision and context provided — do not invent facts, data, or sources not present in the input.

<decision>
{{DECISION}}
</decision>

<context>
{{CONTEXT}}
</context>

Complete these tasks, in order:

1. Identify the 3-5 factors in the provided context most relevant to how this decision could play out (e.g., market conditions, competitive dynamics, regulatory posture, adjacent-industry exposure).
2. Construct exactly 5 distinct scenarios for how this decision could unfold. Each scenario must address all four of the following components, grounded in the provided context wherever possible:
   a. **Market volatility** — price, demand, supply chain, or economic-cycle assumptions
   b. **Competitor responses** — likely moves by rivals, reasoned through basic game-theory logic (how would a rational competitor react to this decision, and how would you likely respond in turn?)
   c. **Regulatory changes** — plausible shifts in law, policy, or compliance obligations
   d. **Black swan events** — one low-probability, high-impact event from an adjacent industry that could cascade into this decision's outcome
3. Assign each scenario a probability percentage. The five probabilities must sum to 100%.
4. For each scenario, state the expected outcome/impact of the decision and list 2-3 early warning signals that would indicate it is starting to unfold.

Before writing your final answer, use the scratchpad below to reason step by step: work through each of the 4 components per scenario, note where the provided context does vs. doesn't give you enough to ground an assumption, sanity-check that your 5 probabilities are mutually distinct and collectively plausible, and only then assign final percentages.

<scratchpad>
[Reason through each scenario's four components, flag any assumptions you're making beyond what the context supports, and draft rough probability weights before finalizing]
</scratchpad>

Uncertainty handling: if the provided context doesn't contain enough information to ground a specific probability, financial impact, or early warning signal, say so explicitly (e.g., "the context doesn't specify X, so this estimate assumes Y") rather than presenting a fabricated figure as fact.

Before presenting your final answer, self-check: confirm the 5 probabilities sum to 100%, confirm each scenario actually addresses all four required components, and confirm every specific claim traces back to either the provided decision/context or an explicitly flagged assumption.

Output format — present exactly 5 numbered scenarios, each with these labeled fields in this order: Scenario Name, Probability, Description (covering all four components), Expected Outcome, Early Warning Signals.
```

---

## Pro tips
- The richer your `{{CONTEXT}}` (recent competitor moves, actual market data, pending regulation), the less the model has to flag as an assumption — thin input means more caveats, not more confidence.
- If two scenarios come back nearly identical, ask a follow-up: "Scenarios 2 and 4 look too similar — sharpen the distinction between them."
- Ask for a comparison table across all 5 scenarios (probability, outcome, top warning signal) as a fast-scan follow-up once you've read the full output.
- Re-run quarterly with updated context — the scenarios should shift as your competitive and regulatory picture changes; a static scenario set is a stale one.

## Example use case
**Entering a new geographic market:** A regional SaaS company preparing to expand into the EU feeds in its expansion decision plus current GDPR enforcement trends, two competitors' recent EU moves, and its own churn data — the model builds scenarios ranging from "smooth regulatory approval + weak local competition" to "surprise data-localization law forces a re-architecture," each with its own trigger points for pulling back or doubling down.
