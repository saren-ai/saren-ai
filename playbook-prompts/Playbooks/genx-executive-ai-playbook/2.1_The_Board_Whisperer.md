# 2.1 Board Whisperer: Transform Reports into Strategic Decks

## What this does
Transforms lengthy operational reports into concise 3-slide executive summaries optimized for board discussions. Emphasizes performance against objectives, risk mitigation progress, and resource requests with ROI projections—while flagging metrics that might trigger difficult questions.

## How to use
1. Copy the entire prompt below
2. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or your preferred LLM
3. Replace `{{OPERATIONAL_REPORT}}` with your operational report or attach the document
4. Hit enter

---

## The Prompt

```
You are an executive communications advisor preparing board-ready materials. You will transform a lengthy operational report into a concise, 3-slide executive summary for a board of directors.

<operational_report>
{{OPERATIONAL_REPORT}}
</operational_report>

Complete this task in the following order:

1. Read the operational report in full.
2. Reason through the report before writing anything, inside a <reasoning> block. In that block:
   - List the stated objectives and your assessment of performance against each one, with the specific numbers that support your assessment.
   - List the risk mitigation efforts mentioned and their current status.
   - List any resource requests and the ROI/payback data attached to each.
   - Flag any metric, trend, or number that could prompt a difficult board question (e.g., a miss, a slowdown, a request without a clear justification).
   - Note anywhere the report simply doesn't provide enough detail — do not guess numbers to fill a gap.
3. Draft the 3-slide executive summary using only what you reasoned through in step 2.

Structure the summary as exactly 3 slides:

**Slide 1: Performance Against Objectives**
- Lead with the most important performance metrics
- Compare actual results to stated targets/objectives
- Include both successes and shortfalls
- Use specific numbers and percentages where available

**Slide 2: Risk Mitigation Progress**
- Highlight key risks that were identified and actions taken
- Show progress on risk mitigation initiatives
- Include any new risks that have emerged
- Quantify risk reduction where possible

**Slide 3: Resource Requests & ROI**
- Present any requests for additional resources (budget, headcount, technology, etc.)
- Include clear ROI projections and payback periods
- Justify resource needs with business impact
- Prioritize requests by strategic importance

After each slide, add a "Board Alert" section that flags any metric, trend, or information from that slide that might trigger difficult questions from board members.

If the report doesn't contain enough information to fill a required element (e.g., no ROI figure was given for a resource request), state that explicitly on the slide ("Not specified in report") and note the assumption you're making instead of inventing a number.

Before presenting your final answer, check your own work: confirm every figure in the summary traces back to the operational report, confirm all 3 slides each have a Board Alert section, and confirm the whole summary uses executive-level language — concise, strategic, focused on business impact rather than operational detail.

Present your final executive summary inside `<executive_summary>` tags, with clear slide headers and bullet points, and nothing outside those tags except your `<reasoning>` block. Close each block with its matching closing tag (`</reasoning>`, `</executive_summary>`) once you finish it.
```

---

## Pro tips
- Attach the raw report as a file if your LLM supports uploads; pasting a huge report as plain text can get truncated in some chat UIs — split into two messages if it's over ~15 pages.
- If your board packet has a strict slide limit, ask the model for presenter notes for each slide in a follow-up prompt rather than cramming more onto the slide itself.
- Run this right after your ops team finalizes a report but before your board deck gets built, so the model's slide-level distillation becomes actual deck prep, not an afterthought.
- If the reasoning block flags more than 2-3 "Not specified" gaps, that's a signal your operational report has a completeness problem, not a prompting problem — fix the report before the board sees the deck.

## Example use case
**Quarterly ops review before the March board meeting:** Paste your 12-page Q1 operations report and get back exactly the version your board actually wants: 3 slides, with a Board Alert flagging the 14% miss on the enterprise renewal target before someone asks about it live.
