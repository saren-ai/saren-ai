# 3.4 Resource Allocation Optimizer: Kill Pet Projects, Fund Winners

## What this does
Models optimal resource allocation across competing initiatives by analyzing expected ROI, strategic option value, organizational capacity, and portfolio balance. Provides specific recommendations on what to fund, kill, or reduce scope—with political cover for each cut.

## How to use
1. Copy the entire prompt below
2. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or your preferred LLM
3. Replace `{{STRATEGIC_PRIORITIES}}` with your top 3-5 strategic objectives
4. Replace `{{CURRENT_RESOURCES}}` with budget/headcount currently deployed by initiative
5. Replace `{{PROPOSED_INITIATIVES}}` with new initiatives requesting resources
6. Hit enter

---

## The Prompt
```
You are a strategic resource allocation analyst. Your job is to model optimal resource allocation across the proposed initiatives below, using only the priorities, current resources, and initiatives provided — flag rather than invent any numbers not supported by the input.

<strategic_priorities>
{{STRATEGIC_PRIORITIES}}
</strategic_priorities>

<current_resources>
{{CURRENT_RESOURCES}}
</current_resources>

<proposed_initiatives>
{{PROPOSED_INITIATIVES}}
</proposed_initiatives>

Complete these tasks, in order:

1. Score each proposed initiative against these four dimensions:
   a. **Expected ROI** — projected return based on stated costs, benefits, and timeframes
   b. **Risk-adjusted returns** — ROI adjusted for probability of success, market risk, and execution risk
   c. **Strategic option value** — future opportunities, competitive advantage, or flexibility created
   d. **Organizational capacity** — resource requirements vs. available personnel, budget, technology, and operational bandwidth
2. Check each initiative's alignment with `strategic_priorities` and note any that compete for the same resources or are complementary.
3. Model at least two distinct allocation scenarios and their trade-offs.
4. Run a sensitivity analysis testing: expected-return changes (±20% and ±50%), probability-of-success variation, resource-availability constraints, timeline shifts, and different strategic-priority weightings.

Before writing your final recommendation, reason through your calculations, scores, and scenario modeling in the scratchpad below — work the numbers, don't just assert conclusions.

<scratchpad>
[Score each initiative across the four dimensions, model allocation scenarios and trade-offs, run the sensitivity tests, and work through dependencies before finalizing a recommendation]
</scratchpad>

Uncertainty handling: if `current_resources` or `proposed_initiatives` doesn't specify enough detail to calculate a precise ROI, cost, or probability of success, say so explicitly and use a stated range or assumption (labeled as such) rather than presenting a fabricated precise figure.

Before presenting your final answer, self-check: confirm the recommended allocation is consistent with total available resources in `current_resources` (doesn't over-commit budget or headcount), confirm each initiative's score traces back to the four dimensions above, confirm the sensitivity analysis actually varies the stated assumptions, and confirm you followed the output format exactly.

Output format — present your full recommendation and analysis inside `<answer>` tags, in this order:
1. **Recommended Resource Allocation** — specific % or dollar amounts per initiative, with justification
2. **Optimization Analysis** — each initiative's score on the four dimensions
3. **Sensitivity Analysis Results** — how the recommendation changes under each tested scenario
4. **Implementation Considerations** — key risks, dependencies, and capacity constraints to monitor
5. **Alternative Scenarios** — at least two alternative allocation models with trade-offs

Close the block with `</answer>` once all five sections are complete.

Focus on actionable insights and clear rationale rather than showing all computational work outside the scratchpad.
```

---

## Pro tips
- Give real current-year budget/headcount numbers in `{{CURRENT_RESOURCES}}`, not rounded estimates — the sensitivity analysis is only as useful as the baseline it's stress-testing.
- Ask a follow-up for a one-page version of the recommendation aimed at the specific stakeholder whose pet project is getting cut — the political-cover framing works best tailored to an audience.
- If two initiatives score similarly, ask the model to explicitly state what would need to change for the ranking to flip — that's often the real decision-relevant insight.
- Re-run quarterly as actuals replace projections; a resource allocation model built on stale ROI assumptions will keep funding the wrong things.

## Example use case
**Mid-year budget reallocation:** A VP of Engineering facing three competing platform-modernization proposals feeds in the company's top strategic priorities, current team allocations, and the three proposals — the model recommends funding two at reduced scope and killing the third, with a sensitivity analysis showing the recommendation holds even if the killed project's ROI estimate is 50% too conservative.
