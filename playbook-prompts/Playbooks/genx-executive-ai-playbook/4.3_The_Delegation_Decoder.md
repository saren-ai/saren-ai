# 4.3 Delegation Decoder: Free Up 15 Hours Weekly

## What this does
Analyzes your current task list against your direct reports' capabilities to identify what you're doing that someone else should own, decisions you're making that should happen at lower levels, and where your involvement adds vs. subtracts value. Creates delegation plans with success metrics.

## How to use
1. Copy the entire prompt below
2. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or your preferred LLM
3. Replace `{{TASK_LIST}}` with your current responsibilities and recurring tasks
4. Replace `{{DIRECT_REPORTS}}` with your direct reports and their strengths/bandwidth
5. Hit enter

---

## The Prompt
```
You are a management consultant specializing in delegation optimization and organizational efficiency. Analyze a manager's current task list against their direct reports' capabilities to identify delegation opportunities and produce actionable plans.

<task_list>
{{TASK_LIST}}
</task_list>

<direct_reports>
{{DIRECT_REPORTS}}
</direct_reports>

Complete the following tasks in order:
1. For every item in the task list, evaluate it against these five criteria:
   - Skill Requirements: what capability, experience, or authority level does it require?
   - Strategic Importance: is this high-level strategic work or operational/tactical work?
   - Development Opportunity: would delegating it help develop a direct report?
   - Manager's Unique Value: does it specifically require the manager's authority, relationships, or expertise?
   - Risk Level: what happens if it's handled incorrectly?
2. Using that evaluation, sort every task into one of: Misaligned Tasks (someone else should own it), Decision Bottlenecks (should be decided at a lower level), Reduce Involvement (manager adds less value than the time cost), or Maintain Direct Involvement.
3. For each task flagged for delegation or a lower decision level, match it to the most appropriate direct report based on their stated capabilities, workload, and development needs.
4. For each match, build a delegation plan: what to delegate, to whom, why (capability + development rationale), how (transition approach and timeline), success metrics, and support needed (training, resources, check-in cadence).

Before producing your final recommendations, work through your reasoning in an analysis block. Go task by task: score each against the five criteria above, note your provisional category, and — for anything you're delegating — your first-pass guess at the best-fit direct report before you finalize the match.

<analysis>
[Work through each task against the five criteria, then your category and delegation match, before finalizing]
</analysis>

If the task list or direct-report data doesn't give you enough detail to confidently assess a task's risk level, skill requirement, or a direct report's fit (e.g., no workload or skill detail provided), say so explicitly next to that item and state the assumption you're using rather than inventing specifics about the person or task.

Before presenting your final answer, check your own work: confirm every task, person, and rationale in your output traces back to the provided task list or direct-report data, and confirm your response follows the exact five-section format below.

Your final output should be organized into these five sections, in order:
1. Tasks to delegate immediately
2. Decisions to push down
3. Items where you should reduce involvement
4. Tasks where you should maintain direct involvement
5. Specific delegation plans (what/to whom/why/how/success metrics/support needed) for every recommended change

Do not include your analysis block in the final response.
```

---

## Pro tips
- List each direct report's current workload, not just their skills — a perfectly capable person who's already at 110% capacity shouldn't get another task dumped on them.
- Include tasks you're not sure about, not just obvious busywork — the five-criteria breakdown often surfaces "invisible" delegation candidates you'd have kept by default.
- Re-run this after every reorg or team change — capability and bandwidth data goes stale fast, and a delegation plan built on last quarter's team is a plan for someone who just left.
- Use the "reduce involvement" category, not just "delegate," for things you should stay looped in on but stop personally executing.

## Example use case
**A director personally approving every expense report and vendor contract:** List out the full task inventory alongside two direct reports' skill sets and current bandwidth — the decoder flags the approvals as decision bottlenecks, assigns contract review to the report with procurement experience, and sets a 30-day check-in as the success metric.
