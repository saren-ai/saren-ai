# 2.4 Crisis Response Architect: Respond in Hours, Not Days

## What this does
Creates rapid response frameworks when competitors make major moves (acquisitions, product launches, pricing changes). Drafts tailored messages for three critical stakeholder groups—employees, investors, and customers—with clear guidance on what to say and what NOT to say.

## How to use
1. Copy the entire prompt below
2. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or your preferred LLM
3. Replace `{{COMPETITOR_MOVE}}` with the specific announcement or action
4. Hit enter

---

## The Prompt

```
You are a strategic communications expert developing a crisis response framework. A competitor has just made a significant move that requires a coordinated response across different stakeholder groups.

<competitor_move>
{{COMPETITOR_MOVE}}
</competitor_move>

Before drafting anything, reason through the situation inside a <reasoning> block:
- Assess exactly what the competitor did and how material it is (pricing, product, funding, acquisition, etc.) based only on what's described above.
- For each stakeholder group below, note the one or two questions that group will actually be asking themselves after hearing this news.
- Identify any fact about your own company's position, financials, or roadmap that this response would need but that hasn't been provided — you cannot invent competitive advantages or financial claims that weren't given to you.

Then draft three distinct response frameworks, one for each stakeholder group:

**Stakeholder Group 1: Employees**
- Primary goal: Build confidence without creating complacency
- Focus on: Internal strength, team resilience, competitive advantages, and continued growth opportunities
- Tone: Motivational but realistic, acknowledging the challenge while emphasizing capability

**Stakeholder Group 2: Investors**
- Primary goal: Demonstrate strategic differentiation and competitive positioning
- Focus on: Unique value propositions, market positioning, financial stability, and strategic advantages
- Tone: Analytical and confident, emphasizing data-driven competitive advantages

**Stakeholder Group 3: Customers**
- Primary goal: Reinforce stability and ongoing value delivery
- Focus on: Continued service quality, reliability, customer-centric improvements, and long-term partnership
- Tone: Reassuring and service-focused, emphasizing commitment and stability

For each framework, provide:

1. **Key Messages** (3-4 main points tailored to that stakeholder group)
2. **Supporting Talking Points** (specific details and evidence to back up each key message)
3. **What NOT to Say** (specific phrases, topics, or approaches to avoid with that stakeholder group)

Guidelines:
- Ensure each framework feels distinct and appropriate for its intended audience
- Key messages should be concise, memorable, and actionable
- Where a "Supporting Talking Point" would normally cite a specific internal metric or advantage you don't have (e.g., exact retention numbers, unannounced roadmap items), write it as a placeholder instruction to the communications team (e.g., "[Insert current NPS/retention figure here]") instead of fabricating a number
- Maintain consistency in overall strategic positioning while adapting tone and emphasis across groups

Before presenting your final answer, check that all three frameworks are internally consistent with each other (none contradicts another on the facts), that every "What NOT to Say" item is specific rather than generic, and that any placeholder data left for the comms team is clearly marked as such.

Close your `<reasoning>` block with `</reasoning>` once you finish it. Present your final response inside `<crisis_response_frameworks>` tags, closed with `</crisis_response_frameworks>`, with a clear heading for each stakeholder group and the three subsections (Key Messages, Supporting Talking Points, What NOT to Say) under each. Do not include your reasoning block in the final output.
```

---

## Pro tips
- Fire this the same day the competitor's move breaks — the whole point is hours-not-days; a stale response framework is worse than none.
- Fill in any `[Insert...]` placeholders the model leaves before this goes to anyone — those exist because the prompt deliberately won't invent your internal numbers.
- Route the Investor framework past your IR/legal team before it leaves the building; forward-looking competitive claims carry disclosure risk the model can't assess.
- Rerun with the same `{{COMPETITOR_MOVE}}` input once you have real internal proof points, and diff the two outputs to see what changed once the placeholders got filled in.

## Example use case
**A direct competitor announces a surprise acquisition of your category's #2 player:** Paste the acquisition announcement in and get three ready-to-adapt frameworks within minutes — an employee message that reframes it as validation of the category rather than a threat, an investor framework leading with your organic growth vs. their acquired growth, and a customer message that doesn't even mention the competitor by name.
