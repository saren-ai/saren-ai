# 4.5 Strategic Alignment Enforcer: Fund What Actually Matters

## What this does
Compares your stated annual objectives against actual resource allocation, leadership time, progress metrics, and incentive structures. Flags objectives getting lip service but no resources, shadow priorities consuming unmarked resources, and success theater vs. real progress—with specific realignment recommendations.

## How to use
1. Copy the entire prompt below
2. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or your preferred LLM
3. Replace `{{ANNUAL_OBJECTIVES}}` with your strategic plan or stated goals
4. Replace `{{RESOURCE_ALLOCATION_DATA}}` with real budget, headcount, and time distribution
5. Replace `{{PROGRESS_METRICS}}` with current results vs. targets
6. Hit enter

---

## The Prompt
```
You are a strategic business analyst evaluating organizational alignment between stated objectives and actual resource deployment. Analyze annual objectives against resource allocation and progress data to identify strategic misalignments.

<annual_objectives>
{{ANNUAL_OBJECTIVES}}
</annual_objectives>

<resource_allocation_data>
{{RESOURCE_ALLOCATION_DATA}}
</resource_allocation_data>

<progress_metrics>
{{PROGRESS_METRICS}}
</progress_metrics>

Complete the following tasks in order:
1. Map each stated annual objective to its corresponding budget, personnel, and time allocation in the resource data.
2. Identify objectives getting lip service but no resources: objectives with minimal or no corresponding allocation.
3. Identify shadow priorities: resource expenditures in the allocation data that don't clearly map back to any stated objective.
4. Identify success theater vs. real progress: objectives where the progress metrics look positive but don't reflect substantive advancement toward the underlying outcome.
5. Develop 3-5 specific, actionable recommendations that address the misalignments found in steps 2-4.

Before producing your analysis, work through your reasoning in a scratchpad. Go objective by objective: what resources map to it, what the progress metrics actually show versus claim, and which of the three misalignment categories (if any) it falls into — then separately list any resource line items that never mapped to an objective in step 1.

<scratchpad>
[Map each objective to resources and progress, categorize misalignments, and list any unmapped resource line items]
</scratchpad>

If the objectives, allocation data, or progress metrics don't contain enough detail to confidently categorize a case (e.g., no dollar figures, no metric history), say so explicitly and state the assumption you're making rather than fabricating specific numbers or outcomes.

Before presenting your final answer, check your own work: confirm every objective, figure, and metric you cite traces back to the provided data, and confirm your response follows the exact format below.

Provide your analysis in the following format:

**STRATEGIC MISALIGNMENT ANALYSIS**

**1. Objectives Getting Lip Service (No Resources)**
For each case: objective name and description, evidence of minimal resource allocation, impact assessment

**2. Shadow Priorities (Unmarked Resource Consumption)**
For each case: description of the shadow priority/activity, resources being consumed, why this represents a misalignment

**3. Success Theater vs. Real Progress**
For each case: objective showing apparent success, metrics used to demonstrate progress, evidence that progress is superficial

**RECOMMENDED REALIGNMENTS**
3-5 specific recommendations, each targeting a specific misalignment above, proposing concrete realignment steps, and estimating impact

Do not include your scratchpad in the final response.
```

---

## Pro tips
- Pull resource allocation from actual budget/headcount systems, not memory — "shadow priorities" only surface when the spend data is real, not estimated.
- Include metrics you're proud of, not just the weak ones — success-theater detection needs to see the metrics that look good on a slide to test whether they hold up.
- Run this before annual planning, not just after a bad quarter — it's most useful for reallocating budget before it's already spent.
- If an objective has no measurable progress metric at all, that's itself a finding — don't wait for the model to infer it; call it out in your input.

## Example use case
**A CEO whose board deck says "AI transformation" is a top-3 priority:** Paste in the five stated annual objectives, the actual departmental budget and headcount breakdown, and this quarter's KPI dashboard — the analysis reveals AI transformation got 2% of budget while a never-announced infrastructure migration quietly absorbed 18% of engineering time.
