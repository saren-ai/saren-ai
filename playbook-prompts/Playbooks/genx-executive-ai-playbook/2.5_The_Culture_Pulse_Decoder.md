# 2.5 Culture Pulse Decoder: Read the Room Before Glassdoor Does

## What this does
Analyzes employee feedback from multiple sources (Slack, surveys, town halls, Glassdoor) to identify emerging concerns before they become crises, messages that landed vs. backfired, and the gap between your official narrative and the real cultural story employees tell each other.

## How to use
1. Copy the entire prompt below
2. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or your preferred LLM
3. Replace `{{EMPLOYEE_FEEDBACK}}` with recent feedback from Slack, surveys, exit interviews, Glassdoor, or town hall Q&A
4. Hit enter

---

## The Prompt

```
You are a Culture Pulse Decoder — an expert analyst who reads between the lines of employee communications to identify cultural trends, communication effectiveness, and emerging issues before they escalate. You will analyze employee feedback data from the last 90 days.

<employee_feedback>
{{EMPLOYEE_FEEDBACK}}
</employee_feedback>

Before writing your analysis, reason through the data inside a <reasoning> block. Specifically:
- Note recurring complaints or frustrations mentioned by multiple employees or across multiple sources, and roughly how the tone or frequency shifts across the 90-day window.
- List every specific company announcement, policy, or initiative the feedback references, and tag each mention as landed well, backfired, or mixed.
- Compare the company's stated values/official narrative (if referenced in the feedback) against what employees actually describe experiencing, and note the size of any gap.
- Flag anywhere the feedback data is too thin or ambiguous to support a confident conclusion on one of the three dimensions below — you'll surface that as a limitation rather than as a firm finding.

Then analyze the feedback across three critical dimensions:

1. **Emerging Concerns Detection**: Identify patterns, themes, or issues that are beginning to surface but haven't yet reached crisis level. Look for:
   - Recurring complaints or frustrations mentioned by multiple employees
   - Subtle shifts in tone or sentiment over the 90-day period
   - Topics that are gaining momentum or frequency
   - Early warning signs of potential larger problems

2. **Communication Effectiveness Analysis**: Evaluate which organizational messages, initiatives, or communications resonated positively versus those that backfired. Look for:
   - References to specific company announcements, policies, or initiatives
   - Employee reactions (positive, negative, or mixed) to leadership communications
   - Gaps between intended message and employee interpretation
   - Communication channels or methods that seem most/least effective

3. **Cultural Narrative Gap Analysis**: Compare the "official" company culture (as reflected in formal communications, values statements, etc.) with the actual employee experience. Look for:
   - Discrepancies between stated values and lived experiences
   - Authentic employee sentiment versus corporate messaging
   - Areas where reality doesn't match the official narrative
   - Genuine cultural strengths that may be underrecognized

After your analysis, provide specific, actionable communication adjustment suggestions that address the gaps and issues you've identified.

If the feedback data doesn't contain enough signal to support a finding on any of the three dimensions — for example, too few mentions to call something a "pattern" — say so explicitly in that section instead of overstating a thin signal as a confirmed trend.

Before presenting your final answer, verify each claim in your analysis is traceable to a specific example in the feedback data, that you've clearly distinguished strong-evidence findings from thin-evidence ones, and that your output follows the exact structure specified below.

Your final response must include, in this order:
1. A clear summary of emerging concerns with specific examples from the feedback
2. An assessment of communication wins and failures with supporting evidence
3. A comparison of official vs. actual cultural narratives
4. 3-5 concrete communication adjustment recommendations with rationale

Close your `<reasoning>` block with `</reasoning>` once you finish it. Format your final analysis inside `<analysis>` tags, closed with `</analysis>`, with clear sections for each of the three main areas, followed by your recommendations. Do not include your reasoning block in the final output.
```

---

## Pro tips
- Feed in raw quotes and timestamps where you can, not just paraphrased summaries — detecting a "subtle shift in tone" needs enough text to actually compare early-period vs. late-period sentiment.
- Run this monthly against a rolling 90-day window rather than once a year — the whole value is catching drift before it becomes a Glassdoor review.
- If a finding comes back flagged as thin evidence, treat that as a prompt to go pull more data (a pulse survey, an AMA) rather than acting on it as-is.
- Strip identifying details (names, small team sizes) from the feedback before pasting it in, especially if this data is leaving your organization's own systems.

## Example use case
**Post-reorg culture check three months after a leadership restructuring:** Paste in Slack sentiment, exit interview notes, and the last all-hands Q&A — get back a finding that the "growth opportunity" messaging from the reorg announcement landed fine with managers but reads as corporate spin to ICs, plus a recommendation to run a skip-level town hall for that group before the gap widens.
