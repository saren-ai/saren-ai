# 2.2 Multi-Generation Translator: One Message, Five Audiences

## What this does
Rewrites important announcements (restructuring, policy changes, strategic pivots) for maximum clarity across your multi-generational workforce. Creates optimized versions for Boomers, Gen X, Millennials, and Gen Z—each addressing their specific concerns and communication preferences while maintaining message consistency.

## How to use
1. Copy the entire prompt below
2. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or your preferred LLM
3. Replace `{{ANNOUNCEMENT}}` with your message or policy change
4. Hit enter

---

## The Prompt

```
You are an internal communications strategist. You will rewrite a corporate restructuring announcement into three versions, each tailored to resonate with a different generational cohort in the workforce, while keeping the underlying facts identical across all three.

<announcement>
{{ANNOUNCEMENT}}
</announcement>

Complete this task in order:

1. Before writing anything, reason through the announcement inside a <reasoning> block:
   - Extract every essential fact, number, date, and commitment in the announcement — this exact list must appear, unchanged, in all three versions below.
   - For each of the three target audiences, note which existing values/concerns (stability vs. growth vs. purpose) the announcement already speaks to, and which it doesn't.
   - Identify any place where the announcement is genuinely ambiguous about scope, timeline, or impact — you'll need to flag rather than paper over these.
2. Using only the facts you extracted, write three distinct versions of the announcement:

**Boomer Executives Version**: Focus on stability, experience, proven track records, and continuity. Emphasize how the restructuring maintains organizational strength and builds on established foundations.

**Gen X Managers Version**: Focus on opportunity, leadership development, practical benefits, and career advancement. Emphasize how the restructuring creates new pathways for growth and leverages their management experience.

**Millennial/Gen Z Staff Version**: Focus on growth potential, purpose-driven work, innovation, and meaningful impact. Emphasize how the restructuring aligns with their values and creates opportunities for professional development and contribution to company mission.

For each version:
- Maintain all essential factual information from the original announcement — do not add facts, numbers, or commitments that aren't in the source
- Adjust tone, language, and emphasis to match the target audience's preferences
- Use terminology and framing that resonates with each generation's typical workplace values
- Keep the core message consistent while adapting the presentation

3. After drafting all three versions, analyze potential misinterpretations:
- Areas where the different versions might create conflicting expectations
- Phrases or promises that could be misunderstood by any audience
- Gaps between what's stated and what might be inferred
- Potential sources of confusion if employees compare versions

If the original announcement doesn't specify something a version would naturally need (e.g., an exact timeline, headcount impact, or who's affected), say so explicitly in that version rather than inventing a specific detail, and flag it again in your misinterpretation analysis.

Before presenting your final answer, verify each of the three versions contains only facts traceable to the original announcement, that no version makes a promise the others contradict, and that your output follows the exact structure below.

Close your `<reasoning>` block with `</reasoning>` once you finish it, then structure your final response with these headings, in this order, and do not include your reasoning block in the final output:

## Boomer Executives Version
## Gen X Managers Version
## Millennial/Gen Z Staff Version
## Potential Misinterpretations
```

---

## Pro tips
- If your actual workforce has a distinct Gen Z-only cohort, ask a follow-up to split the "Millennial/Gen Z" version further — the base prompt treats them as one audience.
- Route the misinterpretation analysis past your HR business partner before sending anything — it's a first-pass risk scan, not a legal review.
- Paste the announcement exactly as employees will receive it (including any FAQ or reference links), so the versions don't accidentally invent details the real announcement omits.
- Use this before a town hall, not after — you want the three versions ready so managers can each speak in the register their team expects.

## Example use case
**Announcing a reporting-line restructuring ahead of Q3:** Paste the 400-word memo about an org redesign and get back three ready-to-send versions — one for the VP layer, one for people managers, one for individual contributors — plus a flagged risk that the memo's "no immediate layoffs" line could be read as "no layoffs ever" by newer staff.
