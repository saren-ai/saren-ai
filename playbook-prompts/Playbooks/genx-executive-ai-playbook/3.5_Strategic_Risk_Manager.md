# 3.5 Strategic Risk Manager: Turn Defense into Competitive Advantage

## What this does
Maps your top strategic risks and models second-order and third-order effects. For each risk, identifies early warning indicators, mitigation strategies that preserve opportunity, and ways to turn defensive moves into competitive advantage—with specific trigger points for action.

## How to use
1. Copy the entire prompt below
2. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or your preferred LLM
3. Replace `{{BUSINESS_CONTEXT}}` with your organization's situation—business model, competitive position, market, and any known risk areas (the model identifies the top 5 strategic risks from this, it isn't just listing back risks you name)
4. Hit enter

---

## The Prompt
```
You are a strategic risk management expert. Your job is to identify the top 5 strategic risks facing this organization from the business context below, and build a complete risk framework for each — grounded in the context provided, not generic industry boilerplate.

<business_context>
{{BUSINESS_CONTEXT}}
</business_context>

Strategic risks are those that could fundamentally impact the organization's ability to achieve its long-term objectives, competitive position, or business-model viability — distinct from day-to-day operational risks.

Complete these tasks, in order:

1. From `business_context`, identify candidate strategic risks and prioritize down to the top 5 most relevant and impactful to this specific organization.
2. For each of the 5 risks, work out: probability, impact, 2nd-order effects (direct consequences if it materializes), and 3rd-order effects (cascading impacts from those consequences).
3. For each risk, define 3-5 measurable early warning indicators (quantitative and qualitative), 2-3 mitigation strategies that reduce exposure without limiting growth, and specific ways proactive management could become a competitive advantage.
4. For each risk, define concrete trigger points for action — both preventive (early intervention) and reactive (damage control) thresholds.
5. After all 5 risks, assess how they interact: risk clusters, cascading scenarios, and overall portfolio risk level.

Before writing your final analysis, use the reasoning space below to work through risk identification and prioritization: what's actually implied by `business_context`, why these 5 rather than others, and how they might compound each other.

<analysis>
[Identify candidate strategic risks from the business context, prioritize the top 5 with reasoning, and note likely interactions between them before drafting the full framework]
</analysis>

Uncertainty handling: if `business_context` doesn't contain enough detail to justify a specific probability, impact rating, or numeric early-warning threshold, say so explicitly and state your assumption (e.g., "context doesn't specify current market share, so this trigger point assumes...") rather than presenting a fabricated figure as fact.

Before presenting your final answer, self-check: confirm each of the 5 risks is actually grounded in `business_context` rather than generic industry risk language, confirm probability/impact ratings have a stated justification, and confirm you've followed the output format exactly.

Output format, in this order:

**STRATEGIC RISK ASSESSMENT**

For each of the 5 risks:
- **Risk [Number]: [Risk Name]**
- **Description** — what it is and why it's strategic (not operational)
- **Probability** — High/Medium/Low with justification
- **Impact** — High/Medium/Low with justification
- **Risk Score** — overall priority ranking explanation
- **2nd Order Effects** / **3rd Order Effects**
- **Early Warning Indicators**
- **Mitigation Strategies**
- **Competitive Advantage Opportunities**
- **Trigger Points for Action**

Then:

**INTEGRATED RISK PORTFOLIO VIEW** — how the 5 risks interact, any clusters or cascading scenarios, and overall portfolio risk level.
```

---

## Pro tips
- The more specific `{{BUSINESS_CONTEXT}}` is about your actual business model and competitive exposure, the less generic the 5 risks will be — thin context tends to produce textbook risk-management language instead of risks specific to you.
- Push back on any risk that reads like it could apply to any company in your industry — ask "how is this specific to our business context, not the sector generally?"
- Ask a follow-up to convert the trigger points into a one-page monitoring dashboard spec (metric, threshold, owner, cadence) for actual operational use.
- Revisit the portfolio view whenever one of the 5 risks' triggers fires — that's exactly when cascading effects from the other risks become live, not hypothetical.

## Example use case
**Pre-board-meeting risk briefing:** A CFO preparing for an annual strategy review feeds in the company's current market position, recent competitive moves, supply chain structure, and regulatory exposure — the model surfaces a supplier-concentration risk as the top strategic risk, models its 2nd/3rd-order effects on margin and delivery commitments, and reframes the mitigation (diversifying suppliers) as a marketing story about resilience versus competitors.
