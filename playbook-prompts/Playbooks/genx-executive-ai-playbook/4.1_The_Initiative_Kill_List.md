# 4.1 Initiative Kill List: Cut 40% of "Strategic" Theater

## What this does
Maps all active initiatives against your strategic pillars and calculates actual resource consumption, progress velocity, dependency bottlenecks, and opportunity cost. Recommends which initiatives to kill with political cover for each cut.

## How to use
1. Copy the entire prompt below
2. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or your preferred LLM
3. Replace `{{STRATEGIC_PILLARS}}` with your 3-5 core strategic objectives
4. Replace `{{INITIATIVE_DATA}}` with all current projects/initiatives, including whatever budget, staffing, and milestone data you have
5. Hit enter

---

## The Prompt
```
You are acting as a strategic Initiative Auditor for an organization. Analyze all active initiatives against the organization's strategic pillars and produce resource-optimization recommendations.

<strategic_pillars>
{{STRATEGIC_PILLARS}}
</strategic_pillars>

<initiative_data>
{{INITIATIVE_DATA}}
</initiative_data>

Complete the following tasks in order:
1. Map every initiative in the data above to one or more of the strategic pillars (or flag it as mapping to none).
2. For each initiative, calculate:
   a. Actual Resource Consumption vs. Plan — compare budgeted resources (financial, human, time) to actual consumption; calculate variance percentages and flag over/under-spending.
   b. Progress Velocity Trend — determine whether milestone completion is accelerating, steady, or decelerating.
   c. Dependency Bottlenecks — identify critical-path dependencies causing delays or resource conflicts with other initiatives.
   d. Opportunity Cost of Continuation — assess what else the same resources could fund, and the relative ROI/strategic fit.
3. Using the four calculations, assess each initiative's overall strategic value and resource efficiency.
4. Build a prioritized "kill list" of initiatives recommended for termination. For each: rationale tied to your calculations, specific supporting data points, diplomatic "political cover" language leadership can use externally, and a resource/team transition plan.

Before you calculate anything, work through your reasoning in a scratchpad. Go initiative by initiative: which pillar(s) it maps to, the raw numbers behind each of the four calculations, and your provisional kill/keep judgment with the single strongest reason. Do this for every initiative before moving to your final write-up.

<scratchpad>
[Work through your per-initiative mapping and calculations here]
</scratchpad>

If the initiative data doesn't include enough detail to calculate one of the four measures for a given initiative (e.g., no budget figures, no milestone history), say so explicitly next to that initiative and state the assumption you're making instead of estimating specific numbers you can't support. Never fabricate a budget figure, date, or outcome that isn't traceable to the provided data.

Before presenting your final answer, check your own work: confirm every dollar figure, percentage, and date in your output traces back to the provided strategic pillars or initiative data (or is explicitly flagged as an assumption), and confirm your response follows the exact output structure below.

Present your final analysis inside `<audit_report>` tags, structured as:
1. A complete mapping table: initiative → strategic pillar(s) → overall verdict (keep/cut/watch)
2. Detailed per-initiative analysis covering all four calculations
3. The prioritized kill list, each entry with rationale, data points, political cover language, and transition plan
4. A summary of total resource savings and reallocation opportunities

Close the block with `</audit_report>` once all four sections are complete.

Do not include your scratchpad in the final response.
```

---

## Pro tips
- Include actual budget/headcount numbers in `{{INITIATIVE_DATA}}`, not just initiative names — the model can't calculate variance without a plan-vs-actual baseline.
- If you're worried about political blowback, ask for two versions of the "political cover" language: one for a board-level audience, one for team leads.
- Run this quarterly against the same pillar list so kill/keep decisions are comparable over time, not just a one-off snapshot.
- Feed in dependency data explicitly (which initiatives share people or budget) — the model can only flag bottlenecks it can see in the data you provide.

## Example use case
**A VP with 14 "strategic" initiatives on the roadmap:** Paste in the three OKRs for the year as pillars and a spreadsheet dump of each initiative's budget, staffing, and last two quarters of milestone dates — the audit flags four initiatives with zero pillar mapping and drafts board-ready language to sunset them.
