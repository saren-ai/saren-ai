# 4.2 Executive Time Audit: See Where Time Actually Goes

## What this does
Analyzes your calendar, email, Slack, and project updates to create a weekly dashboard showing time spent per strategic priority, pending decisions, escalating issues, and where you're the bottleneck. Includes specific reallocation recommendations.

## How to use
1. Copy the entire prompt below
2. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or your preferred LLM
3. Replace `{{DATA_SOURCES}}` with a recent export or summary of your calendar, email, and Slack patterns over the last 90 days (can be a general description if privacy is a concern)
4. Replace `{{STRATEGIC_PRIORITIES}}` with the priorities you want time allocation measured against
5. Hit enter

---

## The Prompt
```
You are building a weekly executive dashboard from multiple data sources. Your goal is to surface actionable insights about time allocation, decision bottlenecks, escalating issues, and where the executive themselves is the bottleneck.

<data_sources>
{{DATA_SOURCES}}
</data_sources>

<strategic_priorities>
{{STRATEGIC_PRIORITIES}}
</strategic_priorities>

Complete the following tasks in order:
1. Extract time-allocation patterns from the data sources: which activities, meetings, and communications consumed time, and roughly how much.
2. Identify every decision visible in the data that has been pending for more than one week.
3. Identify issues that appear to be escalating (recurring, growing, or unresolved across multiple check-ins) without a clear resolution path.
4. Identify situations where the executive appears to be the blocking party — others waiting on their input, approval, or decision.
5. Map the time-allocation findings from step 1 against the stated strategic priorities and flag misalignments (priorities getting less time than their stated importance implies, or vice versa).
6. Develop specific, actionable reallocation recommendations tied to steps 1-5.

Before you draft the dashboard, work through your reasoning in a scratchpad. Work through, in order: the time breakdown by priority (with rough hours or %), the list of stale decisions and what's blocking each, the list of escalating issues and their trajectory, and the list of bottleneck situations — then, only after that, draft your reallocation recommendations.

<scratchpad>
[Work through the four analyses above, in order, before writing the dashboard]
</scratchpad>

If the data sources don't contain enough detail to quantify time allocation precisely (e.g., no meeting durations, no priority tags), say so explicitly and state the assumption or estimation method you used rather than inventing specific hours or percentages.

Before presenting your final answer, check your own work: confirm every hour, percentage, decision, and issue you cite traces back to the provided data sources or strategic priorities, and confirm your response follows the exact section structure below.

Format your response as a professional executive dashboard with these four sections, in this order, followed by recommendations:

**1. Time Spent Per Strategic Priority** — hours/% per priority, direct work plus related meetings, priorities under-served, comparison to ideal allocation where discernible

**2. Decisions Pending >1 Week** — topic, stakeholders, days pending, what's needed to unblock, priority ranking

**3. Issues Escalating Without Resolution** — issue, duration, trajectory/impact if unresolved, resources or actions needed

**4. Where You're the Bottleneck** — who/what is waiting, recurring patterns, urgency and impact

**Reallocation Recommendations** — for each section above, concrete time blocks to shift, decisions to delegate or expedite, issues needing immediate resource allocation, and process changes to reduce future bottlenecks

Do not include your scratchpad in the final response.
```

---

## Pro tips
- The dashboard is only as good as the data you feed it — pull an actual calendar export (or a week-by-week summary) rather than a vague description of "meetings."
- Run this monthly, not just once, so the "where you're the bottleneck" section becomes a trend line instead of a one-time complaint.
- If privacy is a concern with Slack/email content, describe the pattern ("12 Slack threads waiting on my approval") instead of pasting raw messages — the model doesn't need verbatim text to spot the bottleneck.
- Cross-reference the output against your actual OKRs before acting on the reallocation section — the model can only compare time spent to whatever priorities you gave it.

## Example use case
**A CMO drowning in status meetings:** Export the last 90 days of calendar events plus a one-paragraph description of Slack/email response patterns, list the quarter's five strategic priorities, and get back a dashboard showing 60% of hours went to one under-prioritized initiative while two board-critical decisions sat untouched for three weeks.
