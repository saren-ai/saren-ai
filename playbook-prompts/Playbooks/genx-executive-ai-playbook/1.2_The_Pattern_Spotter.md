# 1.2 Pattern Spotter: Identify High-Value Meetings

## What this does
Reviews your recent meeting transcripts to identify recurring patterns—decisions that keep getting deferred, topics that consume time without outcomes, and who actually drives resolution vs. discussion. Creates an "action velocity score" for each participant and recommends structural changes to reclaim wasted time.

## How to use
1. Copy the entire prompt below
2. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or your preferred LLM
3. Replace `{{MEETING_TRANSCRIPTS}}` with transcripts or summaries from your last 6 leadership meetings
4. Hit enter

---

## The Prompt
```
You will be analyzing leadership meeting transcripts to identify patterns in decision-making, discussion dynamics, and participant effectiveness. Your goal is to spot recurring issues and evaluate how different participants contribute to actionable outcomes.

<meeting_transcripts>
{{MEETING_TRANSCRIPTS}}
</meeting_transcripts>

Complete the following tasks, in order:

1. **Deferred Decisions Analysis** — Identify decisions that appear repeatedly across multiple meetings without resolution. For each:
   - Note how many times it appears and in which meetings
   - Identify the stated reasons for deferral
   - Look for patterns in the types of decisions being deferred
   - Determine if the same obstacles keep arising

2. **Time-Consuming Non-Productive Topics** — Identify discussion topics that consume significant meeting time but consistently fail to produce concrete outcomes:
   - Topics that generate lengthy discussion but end without resolution
   - Recurring debates that circle back to the same points
   - Issues that consume time disproportionate to their importance or urgency

3. **Participant Behavior Patterns** — Analyze each named participant to distinguish resolution-drivers from discussion-drivers:
   - Who proposes concrete next steps, deadlines, or solutions
   - Who asks clarifying questions that move discussions forward
   - Who tends to extend discussions without adding actionable value
   - Who successfully pushes for decisions and follow-through

4. **Action Velocity Score** — Score each participant 1-10 using these weighted criteria:
   - Resolution-driving behaviors (proposing solutions, setting deadlines, pushing for decisions): highest weight
   - Quality of contributions (actionable insight vs. general commentary): medium weight
   - Follow-through mentions (references to completing prior commitments): medium weight
   - Discussion efficiency (concise, focused contributions vs. tangents): lowest weight

Before writing your final answer, work through your observations in a scratchpad block. In it:
- Build a running tally, per meeting, of each recurring decision and topic you find, so your frequency counts in the final answer are traceable
- List concrete quotes or paraphrased moments for each participant that justify their behavioral pattern, before you assign a score
- Cross-check your action velocity scores against the weighting above rather than assigning them by gut feel

<scratchpad>
[Work through your analysis here - identify patterns, take notes on each participant, track recurring issues, etc.]
</scratchpad>

If the transcripts don't give you enough turns from a given participant, or enough recurrences of a topic, to support a confident conclusion, say so explicitly rather than inflating the pattern — note it as a "limited sample" observation instead of stating it as a firm trend.

Before presenting your final answer, verify each decision, topic, and score you cite is grounded in something that actually appears in the transcripts above, and that your response follows the output format below exactly.

Structure your final response as follows:

1. **Deferred Decisions Summary**: the top 3-5 decisions that keep getting deferred, with frequency counts and the main reasons for deferral

2. **Time-Sink Topics**: 2-4 topics that consume disproportionate time without generating outcomes

3. **Participant Analysis**: for each participant, provide:
   - Their primary behavioral pattern (resolution-driver vs. discussion-driver)
   - Specific examples of their contributions
   - Their action velocity score with brief justification
   - Key strengths and areas for improvement

4. **Overall Patterns**: the most significant patterns affecting meeting effectiveness

Format your final analysis clearly with headers and bullet points. Focus on actionable insights that could help improve future meeting effectiveness.
```

---

## Pro tips
- Feed at least 4-6 meetings — one or two transcripts won't give the model enough recurrence to spot a real pattern
- Redact anything genuinely sensitive (comp, HR matters) before pasting; nothing here needs that detail to work
- If a participant only speaks a handful of times, expect (and trust) a flagged "limited sample" caveat rather than a confident score
- Re-run quarterly against fresh transcripts to see whether your action velocity scores are actually improving

## Example use case
**After a string of exec meetings:** Paste in 6 weeks of leadership meeting notes → surface that the same pricing decision has been deferred 4 times and see exactly who keeps the room stuck in discussion mode.
