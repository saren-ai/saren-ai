# 2.3 Investor Narrative Builder: Build Cohesive Investment Stories

## What this does
Synthesizes quarterly results, competitor moves, and market dynamics into a cohesive investor narrative that answers "why should I stay invested?" Structures your story as: why we won this quarter, how we're positioned for next quarter, and the moat we're building—with specific proof points and preemptive responses to the bear case.

## How to use
1. Copy the entire prompt below
2. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or your preferred LLM
3. Replace `{{QUARTERLY_DATA}}` with your earnings report or results summary
4. Replace `{{COMPETITOR_ANALYSIS}}` with recent competitor moves and positioning
5. Replace `{{MARKET_DYNAMICS}}` with broader market trends, industry conditions, or macro factors relevant to your narrative
6. Hit enter

---

## The Prompt

```
You are an investor relations strategist. You will synthesize quarterly performance, competitive landscape, and market conditions into one cohesive investor narrative.

<quarterly_data>
{{QUARTERLY_DATA}}
</quarterly_data>

<competitor_analysis>
{{COMPETITOR_ANALYSIS}}
</competitor_analysis>

<market_dynamics>
{{MARKET_DYNAMICS}}
</market_dynamics>

Before writing, reason through the three sources inside a <reasoning> block:
- Pull the specific metrics, growth rates, or wins from the quarterly data that will anchor Section 1.
- Cross-reference the market dynamics and competitor analysis to identify openings, trends, or competitor weaknesses that support Section 2.
- Identify which combination of quarterly performance, market position, and competitive dynamics points to a durable, defensible advantage for Section 3.
- List the most obvious bear-case objections an investor would raise given this data, and how the sources do or don't already answer them.
- Note anywhere one of the three sources is thin or silent on something the narrative would normally need — you'll need to acknowledge that gap rather than fill it with an invented number.

Then write a structured investor narrative with exactly three sections:

**Section 1: Why We Won This Quarter**
- Analyze the quarterly data to identify key performance drivers and wins
- Reference specific metrics, growth rates, or achievements from the quarterly data
- Connect these wins to broader strategic initiatives or market positioning
- Use concrete numbers and percentages where available

**Section 2: How We're Positioned for Next Quarter**
- Draw insights from both the market dynamics and competitor analysis to forecast opportunities
- Identify market trends that favor your company's positioning
- Highlight competitive advantages or gaps in competitor strategies that create openings
- Reference specific market conditions or competitor weaknesses that support your outlook

**Section 3: The Moat We're Building**
- Synthesize information across all three data sources to articulate sustainable competitive advantages
- Explain how current quarter performance demonstrates moat-building activities
- Show how market position and competitive dynamics reinforce long-term defensibility
- Focus on barriers to entry, network effects, switching costs, or other durable advantages

Throughout your narrative:
- Include specific proof points (metrics, data, examples) from the provided sources to support each claim — if a claim would need a proof point that isn't in the sources, say the data isn't available rather than fabricating a figure or citing a study that wasn't provided
- Anticipate and preempt the bear-case objections you identified in your reasoning, acknowledging challenges and explaining how you're addressing them
- Maintain a confident but realistic tone appropriate for investor communications
- Ensure each section flows logically into the next to create a cohesive story

Before presenting your final answer, verify: every metric you cite appears in one of the three provided sources, every bear-case objection you raised is actually addressed, and the three sections read as one unified narrative rather than three disconnected write-ups.

Close your `<reasoning>` block with `</reasoning>` once you finish it. Present your final narrative inside `<investor_narrative>` tags, closed with `</investor_narrative>`. Do not include your reasoning block in the final output.
```

---

## Pro tips
- Feed real GAAP/non-GAAP figures from your earnings deck as `{{QUARTERLY_DATA}}` — the model can only cite proof points that are actually in the source, so a vague summary in produces a vague narrative out.
- If you don't have a formal competitor analysis, a bullet list of recent competitor announcements and pricing moves is enough for `{{COMPETITOR_ANALYSIS}}`.
- Run this before your earnings call script is finalized — the bear-case reasoning is a useful gut-check for what analysts will actually ask.
- Have your CFO or IR lead sanity-check every cited number against source documents before it goes external; this prompt structures the story, it doesn't audit the math.

## Example use case
**Prepping for a Q3 earnings call after a competitor cut prices 15%:** Feed in your quarterly results, a summary of the competitor's pricing move, and a note on softening enterprise demand — get back a narrative that leads with your retention numbers, reframes the competitor's price cut as a signal of weaker margins, and preempts the "are you losing pricing power" question analysts will ask.
